inequality between the phase of `L(s, sym²Δ)` and the phase of `ζ` — **two different L-functions

## Why this is decisive (the structural lesson)

The error the on-ramp tempted us into: conflating *"Euler-borne / DH-lacks"* with *"couples to ζ's
line."* `L(s, sym²f)` is maximally Euler-borne, yet it is a **sibling** L-function, not a **handle on
ζ**. To bound `w_zeros(ζ)` one needs an Euler-borne quantity tied to ζ **by the explicit formula
itself** — i.e. built from ζ's *own* primes `Λ(n)` (the von-Mangoldt prime phase), not from `Δ`'s.
But ζ is GL(1): it **has no sym²** of its own. The sym²/RS structure lives one level up (on a cusp
form), and importing it gives an object whose positivity certifies the *cusp form's* RH, decoupled
from ζ.

This **sharpens the standing wall**: the missing dominator must be **ζ-intrinsic** (built from ζ's
primes, coupling to ζ's zeros via the explicit formula), AND Euler-borne (DH-refusing). The sym²
trick supplies Euler-borne-ness only by changing the L-function — losing the coupling to ζ. So the
two requirements (Euler-borne · couples-to-ζ's-line) cannot both be met by *borrowing* a sibling's
sym²; the coupling `W_∞ ≥ Σ_p W_p` must be forged on ζ's **own** prime phase, where the prime
contribution is **signed** (DONE #0) — exactly the standing wall, now confirmed from one more side.
