## Abstract (plain text, for the arXiv abstract field)

We study the obstruction to importing Weil's function-field proof of the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) into the
number-field setting. We first reduce RH to a single reality condition, F^dagger = conj(F) — the arithmetic
(Rosati) adjoint equals the analytic (complex-conjugation) adjoint — on a polarized arithmetic Hodge
category. We then prove two no-go results. The Geometric No-Go is a structural theorem: a similitude
F . F^dagger = [deg] underdetermines the signature of its invariant Hermitian form, so the positive-
definiteness that is RH is independent of the duality structure and cannot be forced by it; equivalently,
spectra violating RH are consistent objects in every coherence structure (Tannakian, C*, o-minimal, and the
rest), where the positivity is always an external input. The Foundational No-Go shows that a finite additive
characteristic at the archimedean place of Q is contradictory with Q having characteristic 0 (via Ostrowski's
theorem, the definition of characteristic, and the classification of finite semifields); consequently the
finite ground that Weil's proof requires at infinity is not merely missing but forbidden — "archimedean" is
definitionally equivalent to "characteristic 0." The unifying mechanism is a count/radius decoupling: the
reality is multiplicative and free at every place, while the scale q = |F_q| is additive (a characteristic)
and exists only over a genuine finite field. We neither prove nor disprove RH; we establish that if RH is
provable, the proof cannot route through giving the archimedean place a finite ground, and must instead live
on the analytic/characteristic-0 side.

## Cover note (for a journal editor or for sharing)

This paper isolates, and proves, why the program of importing Weil's geometric proof of the Riemann
Hypothesis from function fields to the rational numbers cannot succeed in the form usually sought. The two
results are independent and of different character. Theorem A (the Foundational No-Go) is an elementary but,
to the author's knowledge, not previously formalized observation: the finite ground that Weil's Hodge-index
argument requires at every place is, at the archimedean place of Q, contradictory with Q being a field of
characteristic 0 — it is the definitional identity "archimedean = characteristic 0," with the relaxations to
characteristic-1 semifields and to F_1-bases closed by the finite-semifield classification and an explicit
survey. Theorem B (the Geometric No-Go) is a clean structural statement explaining why every categorical or
analytic reformulation surveyed stalls at exactly the same point: a duality/similitude structure is
signature-blind, so the RH-positivity is never forced by it. The contribution over the founding intuition of
the field-with-one-element program is the formalization-as-contradiction and the exhaustion. The paper does
not claim to prove or disprove RH; it sharpens what a proof must supply, and rules out one major avenue.
